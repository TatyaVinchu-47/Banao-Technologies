## 1. What did you build, and what business outcome does it move?

The tool is in Python and runs in about 2 seconds. It:
1. Reconciles the refund export. It removes the 638 migration duplicates and converts legacy Freshdesk amounts from paise to rupees. A bridge table goes from "export as-is" to the reconciled total and balances every quarter.
2. Reads every refund ticket's customer message and agent note. From the text it decides (a) what the refund was actually for and (b) whether a replacement or reshipment also went out.
3. Writes the board pack: monthly refunds by dropdown code, by actual reason, by team and by agent, plus an exception list.

Arjun's "crore a quarter" is a units bug. The real figure is **Rs 12.80 lakh for Apr–Jun 2026** (Rs 67.1 lakh over 18 months). The helpdesk's Rs 11 lakh counts only `resolved` tickets.

**Business goal: cut refund-plus-replacement (policy §5 says never both) from 16.1% of refunds (76 of 473 last quarter) to under 2% by Q3 FY27 (Oct–Dec 2026). That is worth about Rs 1.2 lakh a quarter, or Rs 5 lakh a year.**

How the saving is calculated:
- Last quarter's 76 confirmed cases cost Rs 1,40,120 (policy cost = product unit cost + Rs 340 each). That is Rs 1,844 per case on average.
- 2% of 473 refunds is about 9 cases, so the target removes about 67 cases: 67 × Rs 1,844 ≈ **Rs 1.24 lakh a quarter**.
- This is the floor. It counts only the replacement that should not have shipped, not the Rs 2.65 lakh refunded on those same tickets.
- The 2% target comes from what Billing (2%) and the Returns Desk (5%) already achieve.

## 2. What does one run cost, and what would a month cost at ~650 tickets/week?

**No paid API calls were used.** The production path is deterministic rules: Rs 0 and about 2 s per run on a laptop.

The optional LLM layer runs llama3.1 8B locally through Ollama, so it also costs Rs 0. It takes about 15 s per ticket on an M2 MacBook Air with 8 GB RAM, and it only sees the review queue.

Monthly volume:
- 650 tickets/week × 4.33 ≈ **2,815 tickets a month**.
- About 20% carry a refund, so **about 565 refund tickets a month**.
- The review queue is about 0.5% of refunds (12 in 2,340), so **about 3 local LLM calls a month**, roughly 45 s of laptop time.
- **Monthly cost: Rs 0** apart from the machine it runs on.

If they wanted a hosted model to second-check every refund ticket, using Claude Haiku 4.5 at $1 / $5 per million input / output tokens ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/claude/haiku)):
- Each call is about 600 input tokens (prompt plus note) and 60 output tokens.
- Cost per call: 600 × $1/1M + 60 × $5/1M = $0.0009.
- 565 calls × $0.0009 = **$0.51 a month, about Rs 45**.
- Running it on all 2,815 tickets would be about $2.50 (Rs 220) a month.

I would not recommend paying for it. See Q3: the small local model was much worse than the rules, and the rules are free.

## 3. How do you know it works?

There are three layers. Everything is in `python3 evaluate.py`.

1. **Arithmetic checks (6 of 6 pass).**
   - The bridge balances in every quarter.
   - All 125 duplicate pairs that carry a refund match exactly once the legacy copy is divided by 100.
   - Duplicate pairs are identical on every other column.
   - Every legacy amount is a multiple of 100.
   - No duplicates remain after cleaning.
   - Ticket-level detail sums to the reconciled total.
2. **Hand-labelled sample of 139 refund tickets** (`eval/gold_labels.csv`).
   - **100 drawn at random (seed 7):**
     - "Refund AND replacement?": the tool agreed on **99/99** decidable tickets. One ticket was genuinely ambiguous, and the tool sent it to review.
     - The helpdesk's Y/N flag agreed on only 87/99. It **caught just 6 of the 18 real double remedies**.
     - "What was the refund for?": the tool matched on 100/100. The agents' dropdown code fit the real reason on only 56/100. 39 of the 50 GW-OTHER tickets had a specific codable reason.
   - **39 targeted hard cases:** flag/note disagreements, self-contradicting notes, and "replacement" mentioned but negated. The tool matched on 38/39. It made **no false "confirmed"**, so it does not overstate leakage. The one miss was sent to review rather than misclassified.
3. **Rules vs LLM on the same 139.** llama3.1 matched 65/100 on the random set and 23/39 on the targeted set. It over-calls "yes", with 40 false positives.

**Honest error rate.** The observed error on the random sample is about 0%. The 95% upper bound on 100 is about 3%. The real figure is likely higher because:
- I wrote the rules, the labels and the checks myself, with AI help. The labels were made by reading the same notes the rules were tuned on, so this is optimistic.
- Nobody at Vireo has validated the labels.
- The fix I propose is in the memo: Neha's team re-checks 50 flagged tickets.

**Where it goes wrong:**
- **Notes that contradict themselves**, e.g. "Replacement dispatched… Refunded. Replacement request rejected as per policy". These go to review.
- **Empty notes** ("as discussed //AIS", "-", "sorted"). The tool falls back to the customer message, then the intake category. It can never confirm a replacement from an empty note.
- **Note templates it has not seen.** It misses them silently.
- **DOA vs in-warranty fault** cannot be told apart from text, so the evaluation merges them.

## 5. What is wrong with what you are handing us?

- **The evaluation is self-graded.** The gold labels were produced by me, with an AI assistant, reading the notes. Same author as the rules. Treat 100% as "no errors found in 100", not "no errors".
- **The rules are tuned to this export's note templates.** They are regex plus fuzzy spelling correction. A new template or a new abbreviation will be missed silently. No drift alarm exists.
- **The month is the ticket creation month, not the refund date.** No refund date exists in the data. Open and pending tickets with a refund raised are included, so some may never have been paid.
- **Field vs note amounts.** The amount used is the `refund_amount_inr` field, even when the note says something else. TK-247254's note says "Rs 500 initiated" and then "Refunded Rs 2499". I did not reconcile note amounts against the field.
- **The leakage cost model is simple.** Unit cost + Rs 340 per case. Part-only replacements (strap, case) are costed at Rs 340, but only 2 of them exist. Partial refunds are not netted off. Refurbishment recovery is excluded, as policy says.
- **The roster join is untested.** The date-valid roster join is written for multiple assignments per agent, but this roster has one row per agent, so that path has never actually been exercised.
- **The cross-ticket match is noisy.** It links a refund on one ticket to a replacement on another ticket for the same order. It is in the CSV but excluded from totals and from review. Most of its 327 matches link unrelated issues.
- **`QUARTER_FROM = "2026-04"` is hard-coded** for the "last quarter" sheets.
- **The paise conversion rests on evidence, not confirmation.** It is based on data evidence plus Sameer's email. Finance has not confirmed it.
- **`customers.csv` in the pack is a 110 MB PNG**, not a CSV. It is unused. If customer-level analysis is needed (e.g. Care+), it cannot be done from this pack.
- **No unit tests.** `evaluate.py` is the only regression check.

## 6. What did you deliberately leave out, and why that rather than something else?

Left out:
- **SLA breach credits (Rs 350 each).** They are probably a bigger rupee line than the leak. They are not refunds, though, and Arjun asked about refunds. Adding them would have meant fixing the IST/UTC timestamp issue on legacy `resolved_at` and the channel targets. That is a separate analysis and I named it in the memo.
- **Repeat contacts / FCR, transfers, staffing.** These are out of the brief.
- **A dashboard or UI.** Arjun wants something to "drop into the board pack". An Excel workbook does that, a web app doesn't.
- **A hosted LLM.** The rules beat the local model by a wide margin. Sending customer messages to a third party needs Vireo's agreement, and nobody was available to ask.
- **Customer-level analysis.** The customers file is unusable (see Q5).

Why the double-remedy leak rather than "GW-OTHER over-use" as the money goal: GW-OTHER is a *labelling* problem. Fixing it changes the report, not the cash. Refund plus replacement is cash leaving the business against written policy, it was invisible in the flag, and it can be fixed with one helpdesk validation rule.

## 7. Anything you built or found that nobody asked for?

- **The replacement flag is wrong two times in three when it matters.** 199 of 360 confirmed double remedies are flagged N. Neha's "probably one-offs" came from a sample that relies on that flag.
- **It is not the Returns Desk.** Its double-remedy rate is 5%. Escalations & Warranty is at 59%, Chat at 28%, Logistics at 27%. One Logistics agent (A3030) accounts for 48 of the 360.
- **Product-fault refunds** (policy says repair or replace) rose from Rs 0.6 lakh to Rs 2–2.6 lakh a quarter from Q4 2025.
- **CSAT is flat at about 3.5** across all six quarters. Excluding blanks, as policy says, the +0.4 Priya cites is not in the data. If blanks are counted as zero, the average is about 1.6, so a mis-averaged report could invent movements.
- **Two agents sign notes as "KS"** (A3003 and A3017). Attribution must use `agent_id`, as the README warns.
- **879 GW-OTHER refunds are above the Rs 500 goodwill cap.** Most are mis-coded rather than real goodwill, which is itself a finding about the dropdown.
- `customers.csv` is a PNG (see Q5).
